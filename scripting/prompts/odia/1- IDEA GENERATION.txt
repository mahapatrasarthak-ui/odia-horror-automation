- A short English explanation may be used only when the requested output field explicitly asks for an internal production note.

- Use a fictional locality by default. If a real place, event, person, community, ritual, or historical claim is named, it must be real and verifiable.
- Distinguish observed cultural detail from invented supernatural fiction.
- Do not present a tribal, religious, caste, regional, or occupational identity as inherently evil, primitive, or dangerous.
- Do not use living sacred places or communities as disposable horror props.
- Prefer specific environmental details: laterite or concrete roads, paddy fields, coastal rain, river crossings, old town lanes, hostels, markets, workplaces, hill roads, or urban apartment blocks when the premise supports them.

STORY RULES

- Every idea needs a strong opening hook and a relatable human problem.
- Make the supernatural rule clear enough to sustain a complete short episode.
- Favour concepts that can become numbered parts without repeating the same twist.
- Keep the emotional centre human: guilt, grief, betrayal, fear of exposure, family duty, loneliness, or impossible choice.
- Original supernatural plots are fiction. Never present invented events as real news.
